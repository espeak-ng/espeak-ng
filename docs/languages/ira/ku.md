# Kurdish (Kurmanji)

Kurdish (Kurmanji) is the northern variety of Kurdish and is spoken in regions of
Turkey, Syria, Iraq and Iran.  It uses the Latin‑based Kurdish alphabet and has
a relatively rich consonant inventory and a system of eight core vowels.  The
phonological description below is based on reference sources for the Kurmanji
dialect and follows the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA).

## Vowels

Standard Kurmanji has eight phonemic vowels.  These are shown with their
orthographic representation and IPA value:

| Orthography | IPA |
|---|---|
| a | /ɑː/ |
| e | /ɛ/ |
| ê | /eː/ |
| i | /ɪ/ |
| î | /iː/ |
| o | /oː/ |
| u | /ʊ/ |
| û | /uː/ |

Front rounded vowels such as ➨ö➩ and ➨ü➩ are not part of core Kurmanji.  The
Kurdish phonology literature notes that /øː/ (written ➨ö➩) and /ʉː/ (written
➨ü➩) occur in the southern dialect (Xwarîn) and are generally limited to
loanwords.  In Kurmanji these loans often merge with the long back
vowels /oː/ or /uː/; however, in eSpeak NG they are represented using
front‑rounded vowel phonemes (`I:` for ➨ö➩ and `y` for ➨ü➩) to preserve
pronunciation where possible.

## Diphthongs

Kurmanji uses a number of diphthongs formed with /w/ or /j/.  Common examples
include ➨ew➩ /əw/, ➨aw➩ /ɑːw/, ➨ay➩ /ɑːj/ and ➨ey➩ /ɛj/.  These diphthongs are
handled in the spelling‑to‑phoneme rules and dictionary and therefore do not
require separate vowel definitions.
